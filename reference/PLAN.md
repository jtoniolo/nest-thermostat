## 1. Where you stand

On **25 October 2025** Google switched off the cloud for Nest Learning Thermostat gen 1 and gen 2.

**Gone:** app control, remote access, notifications, Google Assistant, Home/Away Assist, Nest Protect HVAC shut-off, all future updates.

**Still working:** the thermostat itself. It still runs your saved schedule, still heats and cools, and you can still turn the dial. Google deleted the device from your account, so no API — official or unofficial — can see it any more.

**One thing worth knowing:** it is still on your WiFi, and it is still uploading logs to Google. Google confirmed this in November 2025. You can block it at the router today, for free.

---

## 2. Short answer

**The flash is unavoidable. What answers the phone afterwards is entirely yours.**

Flashing the NoLongerEvil firmware over USB is the only way in — no software or network trick works, and that isn't a close call. For a gen 2 it's **no soldering and no opening the case**: pull the display off the wall, plug in a micro-USB cable.

After that the device just speaks **plain HTTP to an IP address you choose**. No TLS, no certificates, no auth required. Three endpoints. Point it at his server, or at one you write — and switch between them with a single HTTP POST.

**Writing your own is a real option, not a stretch goal.** The protocol core is ~2,500–3,000 lines, the spec is 2,873 lines of properly-researched documentation, and there's an MIT reference implementation to check yourself against. A day or two, not weeks. And nobody has yet built it as a Home Assistant custom integration — everything today is a server in a container plus a thin client that polls it.

Optionally add a **$25 wire-tap board** for independent ground truth on what the furnace is actually doing.

## 4. Why A works (the one-paragraph version)

The thermostat's first move on boot is to ask `frontdoor.nest.com` where to connect. Google now looks at the model string, sees `Display-2.8`, and sends back **empty URLs**. That's the whole block — one string comparison. If you could answer that request yourself, the thermostat would come back to life.

You can't, because the request is over HTTPS and the device only trusts certificates from its built-in list, which you can't add to. But the chip inside — a TI Sitara AM3703 — will boot **unsigned code over USB** if you hold the display down while powering it. That flaw is baked into the silicon. It was published at Black Hat in 2014 and cannot be patched. That's the way in.

NoLongerEvil uses it to add its own certificate and a `/etc/hosts` entry, so the thermostat talks to your server instead. Everything downstream is easy — the protocol is plain JSON with HTTP Basic auth, and it's fully documented publicly.

**Note on "custom firmware":** it isn't a rewrite. It's Google's own firmware with a boot script and a certificate added. That's a good thing — the display, dial, sensors, learning and relay control are all still Google's code, and they still work.

## 5. Option A — full procedure

### 5.1 What you need

- A **Linux or macOS** machine. **Windows does not work**, not even WSL — the USB timing window is too tight.
- A **data-capable micro-USB cable**. Charge-only cables will not work. This trips up a lot of people.
- Docker on whatever box runs Home Assistant (or HA OS with the official add-on).
- Your MQTT broker details.
- 30–60 minutes. Do it on a mild day, not in a cold snap.

### 5.2 Before you touch anything

Run `scripts/00-preflight.sh` (included). It:

- port-scans the thermostat and saves the result
- photographs your settings for you to copy down
- checks your laptop has libusb and the udev rule
- checks Docker and your MQTT broker

**Write down, by hand, from the thermostat's menu:** serial number, current schedule, heat/cool setpoints, system type, and which wires are connected. Take photos of the screens. You will want these.

**Also worth doing:** a 60-second nmap on the stock unit. Nobody has ever published a port scan of an unflashed gen 2. Almost certainly nothing is listening. If port 8080 answers, tell someone — that would be news.

### 5.3 Flash it

1. Install the NoLongerEvil installer on your Mac/Linux box (Electron app, from GitHub releases).
2. **Charge the thermostat first.** Leave it on the wall for an hour. Below 3.6V the WiFi radio won't even come up, and a flat 14-year-old battery makes the USB step flaky.
3. Turn off power to the furnace at the service switch. Not strictly required — the display comes off the wall — but it costs nothing.
4. Seat the display on its backplate for **2–3 seconds**. This arms the DFU trigger.
5. Pull the display off.
6. Plug in the micro-USB **immediately**. The port is on the back of the display head. No screws, no prying.
7. **Press and hold the display for 10–15 seconds** until it reboots into DFU.
8. The installer picks up a TI device (USB `0451:d00e`) and flashes.

**If it times out** — the single most common problem, and it's a timing/cable issue, not a broken device:

- Try a different cable. Then another one.
- Try a different USB port. Prefer a direct port over a hub.
- Charge longer.
- Repeat steps 4–7. It often takes several goes.

**You cannot brick it.** The recovery path is in the chip's ROM, not on the flash. A failed write just means you try again.

### 5.4 Run the server yourself

Do **not** point it at nolongerevil.com. Self-host. The code is MIT and published.

Use the included `scripts/docker-compose.yml`. The parts that matter:

```yaml
environment:
  API_ORIGIN: "http://192.168.1.50:8000"   # your server's LAN IP. Must be an IP, not a hostname.
  MQTT_HOST: "192.168.1.10"                # your broker
  MQTT_DISCOVERY_PREFIX: "homeassistant"
ports:
  - "8000:8000"    # thermostat talks here
  - "8082:8082"    # web UI + control API
```

Then `docker compose up -d`.

In the installer wizard, choose **Self-Hosted** and confirm the detected address. Reboot the thermostat (hold the display 10 seconds). It sends its full state up and appears in Home Assistant automatically via MQTT discovery.

If you run HA OS, there's an official add-on instead — same thing, less fiddling. It needs the Mosquitto add-on.

### 5.5 What you end up with

A `climate` entity with current temperature, humidity, setpoint, mode, HVAC action, plus occupancy and battery. Full read and write. All local.

**One asterisk on "fully offline":** weather is proxied to `weather.nest.com`. With no internet, the device works fine but loses weather and anything derived from it.

### 5.6 Lock it down

Once flashed, block the thermostat's outbound internet at your router except for `weather.nest.com` (or block that too and accept no weather). It no longer needs Google for anything, and the log uploads stop.

## 6. Option B — the wire tap (do this as well)

This is not really an alternative. It's insurance, and it's the only way to know what your furnace is *actually* doing rather than what the thermostat says it's doing.

**Idea:** a small ESP32 board behind the thermostat or at the air handler that (a) watches the 24V call wires and (b) can force a call with relays wired in parallel.

### Sensing

One AC optocoupler per wire (W, Y, G, O/B, W2), each measured **against C, not R**.

- **LTV-814** — $0.51 each, 5000V isolation, 4-pin. Use a **10 kΩ** resistor in series on the input.
- Pull-up 10 kΩ to 3.3V on the output, plus **4.7 µF to ground**. Without that capacitor the 60 Hz waveform makes the signal chatter 120 times a second.
- In ESPHome: `inverted: true` and `delayed_off: 300ms`.

Total load on your furnace transformer: about **0.3 VA**. Nothing.

### Control (only if you want HA to drive the heat)

Wire relay contacts **in parallel** with the Nest's own contacts — COM to R, NO to W.

- Relay off (ESP dead, WiFi down, HA rebooting) → thermostat has full control, exactly as if your board wasn't there. **Fail-safe by wiring, not by software.**
- Relay on → forces a call.
- Limitation: HA can force ON, never force OFF.

**Do not** use the transfer/SPDT pattern unless you have a **C wire** landed. Without C, the Nest charges itself by pulling current through the heat wire, and cutting that path makes it pulse the heat on and off. That is a real, documented failure.

### Rules you must not break

- **Never let a relay connect R to C.** That's a dead short across the transformer.
- Put a **0.5 A polyfuse** on your R tap. It blows before the furnace board's 3 A fuse does, and saves you a trip to the basement.
- **Never energise Y without G.** Compressor without a blower freezes the coil.
- **Compressor minimum-off timer must live on the ESP, not in HA.** 5 minutes minimum off, 5 minutes minimum run. If HA restarts mid-cycle you can kill a compressor.
- Interlock W and Y so they can never both be on.
- Check the Rh/Rc jumper first. If it's missing you have two transformers, and bridging them destroys both.
- 24VAC is Class 2 — no shock risk. The risk is to the equipment.

### Power

R and C **are** the 24VAC supply. Rectified, that's about **34–38 V** — right at the edge of most cheap buck modules. Use a **Recom R-78HB5.0-0.5** (9–72V in, ~$9) or a bridge + 50V capacitor + LM2596 **set to 5.0V on the bench before you connect the ESP**. Do not use a 7805 — 15% efficient, 5.6 watts of heat inside a wall box.

No C wire? Don't power-steal. Pull new cable, or mount the board at the air handler where R, C, W, Y, G all land anyway. That's what most people do and it's easier to work on.

### Room temperature

Add your own sensor. Do **not** put it in the box with the ESP32 — WiFi and the regulator will read 2–5 °C high.

- **SONOFF SNZB-02D**, $12.99, Zigbee, has a display so a human can sanity-check it. Put it in the room you actually care about.
- **DS18B20**, $3, on a 60 cm lead, at the old thermostat location for continuity.
- Bonus: one DS18B20 in the supply plenum and one in the return. Supply-minus-return is the single most useful HVAC diagnostic you can buy for $5. A falling delta-T means a dirty filter, low charge, or a failing coil.

### Bring it up in stages

Sensing only, for a week. Confirm the state machine matches what the Nest is doing. **Then** wire the control relays.

## 7. Option D — write your own server

### 7.1 Scale of the job

Smaller than it looks.

**What his server is:** Python 3.11+, **aiohttp**, aiosqlite, aiomqtt, SQLModel/SQLAlchemy/Alembic, pydantic. MIT. ~11,000 lines over 59 files, plus 2,600 lines of tests. But most of that is a web dashboard, MQTT discovery, API keys, device sharing and pairing. **The protocol core is `transport.py` — 1,012 lines.** A minimum viable device-facing server is **2,500–3,000 lines**.

**Three endpoints:** `/entry`, `/subscribe`, `/put`.

**Two things that shrink the job a lot:**

- **Plain HTTP on the LAN.** No TLS, no certs, no PKI. TLS is optional in his server and off by default. (Watch [issue #102](https://github.com/codykociemba/NoLongerEvil-Thermostat/issues/102) — some firmware builds may still attempt HTTPS. Confirm against your build.)
- **Auth is optional.** `REQUIRE_DEVICE_PAIRING` defaults to false. The protocol doc's own advice: accept every request, never return 401, just parse the serial out of the Basic Auth username (`d.{SERIAL}.{suffix}`).

**The spec is real.** `cjserio/nest-thermostat-protocol-docs` — 2,873 lines, revision 2.9, February 2026, MIT. Not a hand-wave: an implementation checklist, an "avoid" list, per-field access modes, a state-interaction matrix, and a changelog that documents *corrections* to earlier revisions. That last part matters — it means it was written against a real device, not guessed.

**Honest estimate: a day or two of writing.** The real cost isn't the code, it's that you have exactly one device to validate against and you can't start until you've flashed.

### 7.2 Integration or add-on

Both install fine. HACS custom repo for an integration; Supervisor custom repository URL for an add-on. You're on HA OS, so both are open to you.

**The only differences that actually matter:**

| | Custom integration | Add-on |
|---|---|---|
| **Entities** | Native, direct, `local_push` | Needs MQTT (or a companion integration) to get state into HA |
| **Python environment** | Shares HA's | Its own |
| **Dashboard** | Build it as HA entities | Ingress gives you a web UI for free |

Everything else people list — restarts, port binding, connection drops — is a wash. Add-ons are containers too; updating one restarts it, and a host reboot restarts everything either way. The thermostat reconnects in under a second regardless, and it runs its schedule locally the whole time.

**One real constraint on the integration path:** HA pins aiohttp centrally and ships its own SQLAlchemy for the recorder. Declaring SQLModel + Alembic + SQLAlchemy in `manifest.json` can fight HA's resolver. **Fix: don't use an ORM.** One thermostat's state is a few KB — raw `aiosqlite`, or a JSON file under `hass.config.path()`. Five-minute decision, then the constraint is gone.

**Recommendation: integration.** It deletes the MQTT hop and the second container, and you get native entities with a config flow. Nobody has built this — every existing project is a server in a container plus a thin HA client that polls it or reads MQTT.

**Can an integration bind its own port?** Yes. `emulated_hue` and `homekit` do exactly this in core, using the same `AppRunner` / `TCPSite` pair his server already uses in `main.py`. It's a port, not a rewrite.

**Best of both:** write the server as a **plain Python module with no HA imports**, then wrap it twice — once as an integration, once as an add-on. Cheap, and it means the protocol code is testable on its own.

### 7.3 Protocol details worth knowing before you write a line

All documented, all cost a day each if you discover them the hard way. These apply to **any** implementation, including his:

- **JSON field order is load-bearing.** `object_revision` and `object_timestamp` must serialise before `object_key`. His code relies on Python dict insertion order and the docstring says so.
- **The PUT response is a write receipt.** Return `{object_revision, object_timestamp, object_key}` only. Include a `value` field and the device applies everything in it as authoritative cloud data.
- **The advertised URL must carry an explicit port**, even for 80/443. Otherwise the device's parser falls back to a stale cached port, WoWLAN keepalive is misconfigured, and server push can never wake a sleeping device.
- **Chunked transfer, and the server drives the reconnect.** Hold ~290 s, send `X-nl-suspend-time-max: 300`, close by writing the `0\r\n\r\n` terminator. Go past ~350 s and the device abandons the socket without closing it — you get overlapping subscriptions.
- **Do not enable `SO_KEEPALIVE`.** The device can't answer probes with its CPU asleep.
- **Schedule day 0 is Monday.** Temperatures are always Celsius. Times are seconds from midnight.

### 7.4 Gaps in the spec

The doc covers the transport layer and the thermostat data model well enough to build from without capturing traffic. It does **not** cover `weather_url`, `upload_url`, `pro_info_url` or `/info` — read those out of his MIT-licensed source rather than reversing them. It marks its own auth section "provisional" and error handling "partial"; `if_object_revision` conflict semantics are explicitly implementation-defined.

Doc + his source together are complete.

### 7.5 How it actually wires together

**Direction of travel:** the thermostat is a pure client. It dials out, holds a long-poll, sleeps its CPU, and gets woken by your push via WoWLAN. Your server never dials it. So the integration binds a socket and accepts inbound connections — which is allowed, and is exactly what `emulated_hue` and `homekit` do in HA core.

Don't hang it off HA's own web server on 8123. That's authenticated and a 2012 thermostat can't do bearer tokens. Bind your own port.

**The port reaches the device through two separate channels.**

*Once, out of band:* the flashed device runs a small HTTP API on **its own port 8080**. POST to `/cgi-bin/api/settings` with `{"endpoint": "http://192.168.1.50:9543"}`. That is the only thing you ever tell it by hand.

*Every time after:* the device POSTs `/entry` to that address and your server replies with the rest of the map — `transport_url`, `czfe_url`, `ping_url`, `weather_url`, `upload_url` — all absolute URLs you generate from your own config, pointing back at yourself.

**So the config flow needs three fields, not one:**

| Field | Default |
|---|---|
| Bind address | `0.0.0.0` |
| Bind port | `9543` |
| Advertised base URL | auto-detected LAN IP + port, user-overridable |

Bind and advertised are deliberately separate. Two rules on the advertised one: **an IP, not a hostname**, and **an explicit port always**, or the device's parser falls back to a stale cached port and push can never wake it.

**Picking the port automatically:** probe a candidate list starting at 9543 and take the first free one — but **persist it in the config entry**. Do not use an ephemeral port (bind 0); it would change every restart and orphan the device each time. 9543 is the historic Nest transport port and dodges 8123, Mosquitto and the usual add-on suspects.

For the advertised IP, use `async_get_source_ip` from `homeassistant.components.network` — the same helper zeroconf and homekit use for "what address do I announce".

**Finding the device**, in order:

1. **Subnet sweep on port 8080.** Concurrent `GET /cgi-bin/api/settings` across the subnet, 2 s timeout — a /24 in about two seconds. Anything that answers is a flashed Nest, and the body tells you whether it's already pointed at you. Get the subnet from HA's `network` integration rather than assuming /24, and let the user type a CIDR.
2. **Confirm by MAC.** Nest Labs' OUI is `18:B4:30`. Check the ARP entry for each hit. Turns "something answered" into "that's a Nest".
3. **DHCP matcher** in `manifest.json` — `"dhcp": [{"macaddress": "18B430*"}]` — gives you a free "discovered, configure?" card. Only fires on a lease renewal, so it's a bonus, not the mechanism.
4. **Manual IP entry.** Always offer it. Sweeps fail on segmented networks.

**No mDNS.** The device advertises nothing. Avahi is in the GPL source tree and you have root, so you *could* drop a service file in `/etc/avahi/services/` — but the CPU sleeps and a multicast query probably isn't in the WoWLAN wake pattern set, which would likely explain NLE's "mDNS can fail for Nest devices" warning. Not worth a persistent device change that won't survive a reflash, to optimise a step you only do once.

**Verify by waiting.** After pushing the endpoint, wait for `/entry` to arrive. If it doesn't within ~60 s, fail the config flow with a real error. You get a genuine confirmation signal for free, because the device is the one calling you.

**Four operational consequences:**

- **Re-push on port change**, automatically, from the options handler.
- That 8080 API **locks behind a password ~30 minutes after boot**. Sequence the flow as *reboot → sweep → push* and you get an unlocked API and a fresh `/entry` from one action.
- The endpoint change **may not take effect until the device reboots** (NLE tells you to hold the display 10 seconds after configuring). Test which it is — it changes whether the config flow waits or instructs.

### 7.6 How to de-risk it

Flash, run **his** server first, get it working. Then point the device at yours. The endpoint is one field on the device's local port-8080 API, so switching over is a single HTTP POST and switching back is another.

His server becomes your reference implementation, your test oracle and your rollback. Don't build blind against a device you haven't flashed yet.

## 8. What does not work, and why

Save yourself the weekend.

**The Nest web API — badnest, nest_legacy, python-nest, homebridge-nest, nest-api.** These all log in *as you* and ask the cloud what's on your account. Google deleted your gen 2 from the account, so there should be nothing to return.

`tronikos/nest_legacy` is active, well made, and its README still lists "1st, 2nd gen" with **no mention anywhere of the October 2025 shutdown**. I could not find a single report of anyone running it against a gen 1/2 since. The mechanism says it can't work. **But the test costs ten minutes and nothing else — install it via HACS, authenticate, see if the thermostat appears. Do that before you flash.** If it works, everything below is moot and it's news worth reporting.

**Google's SDM API and the built-in HA Nest integration.** Same reason — the device isn't in your account. The $5 Device Access fee buys you nothing here.

**DNS redirect / fake cloud with no flashing.** The idea is right, the execution is impossible. The thermostat validates the server's certificate against a fixed trust store you cannot write to without root. Root needs USB. Nobody has ever demonstrated a network-only redirect.

**OpenWeave / Weave.** A category error you will see repeated on forums. Weave arrived in 2013 with Nest Protect. Your thermostat is from 2012 and doesn't speak it. Its protocol is plain JSON over TLS. OpenWeave was archived in December 2023 anyway. Ignore anyone who suggests this.

**Sniffing the network.** The thermostat is a pure client — it never listens, only dials out. No open ports, no mDNS, nothing to scrape. It does still upload logs to Google, but that's outbound TLS you can't read.

**The 802.15.4 radio.** The hardware really is in there (Ember EM357). It was never enabled. Nobody has brought it up. Don't count on it.

## 10. Risks, honestly

| Risk | Reality |
|---|---|
| Bricking | **Very low.** The recovery path is in silicon. A bad flash is a retry, not a paperweight. |
| Can't get into DFU | **Likely, at least once.** The main annoyance. Cable, port, charge, patience. |
| NLE project abandoned | Mostly one person, pace slowed after spring 2026. **Largely neutralised if you write your own server** — the firmware is the only part you depend on, and it's already on the device. Self-host from day one, keep a copy of the repo and image. |
| Firmware is 99% Google's code | True, and legally grey. Also why it works so well. |
| Trust store expires | Long-term, plausible. The device's certificate list is from 2012 and will never be updated. If Google rotates `weather.nest.com`, weather stops. Self-hosting means nothing else breaks. |
| Battery dies | **The most likely real failure over the next few years.** Fourteen-year-old Li-ion. It is user-replaceable (~568 mAh, 3.7V). Buy a spare. |
| HA integration friction | Expect some with the existing third-party HA clients — unresolved 500-errors and missing-integration reports across early 2026. Another argument for owning that layer yourself. |
| Some firmware builds try HTTPS | [Issue #102](https://github.com/codykociemba/NoLongerEvil-Thermostat/issues/102) is unresolved. Default is plain HTTP; confirm against your build before you write a server that only speaks HTTP. |

## 11. What to build with Claude Code

**1. Your own server, as a HA custom integration.** The main event. Shape:

```
custom_components/nest_local/
  manifest.json      domain, version (required for custom), iot_class: local_push,
                     requirements: []  <- keep this empty or near-empty
                     dhcp: [{"macaddress": "18B430*"}]
  hacs.json          name; consider persistent_directory for state
  __init__.py        async_setup_entry: start aiohttp AppRunner + TCPSite
                     async_unload_entry: close held sockets, site.stop(), runner.cleanup()
  config_flow.py     detect LAN IP -> pick free port -> sweep :8080 -> push endpoint
                     -> wait for /entry or fail. Options handler re-pushes on change.
  discovery.py       subnet sweep, ARP/OUI check, manual entry fallback
  server/            <- NO home assistant imports in here
    entry.py         POST /entry -> URLs pointing back at us
    subscribe.py     chunked long-poll, ~290s hold, X-nl-suspend-time-max: 300
    put.py           write receipt ONLY, never echo a value field
    buckets.py       device / shared / structure / user / schedule
    store.py         aiosqlite or plain JSON. No ORM.
  climate.py         the entity, driven by a push callback from server/
  sensor.py, binary_sensor.py
```

Rules: no ORM, no blocking calls in the event loop, and `server/` must be importable and testable without Home Assistant. That last one is what lets you wrap the same code as an add-on later if you ever want to.

**2. A replay harness.** Before you write the server, run his, and capture the traffic. Then replay it at yours. This is the difference between a day and a week — you get a regression test instead of a thermostat you have to keep walking over to.

**3. Wire-tap firmware.** ESPHome YAML, starter in `esphome/hvac-tap.yaml`. Optocoupler inputs with debounce, a template sensor deriving the true HVAC action, parallel relay outputs with interlock and minimum-cycle timers, and a local DS18B20. The real work is tuning to your wiring — measure every wire against C, idle and calling, before you write a line.

**4. Cross-check automation.** Compare what the thermostat *claims* against what the wire tap *sees*. Alert on: a call active for N minutes with the thermostat idle (welded relay), the thermostat calling with no wire activity (dead relay or blown fuse), short-cycling, a stale sensor, or the thermostat going quiet. This turns two mediocre signals into one trustworthy one.

Source of truth: `cjserio/nest-thermostat-protocol-docs` for the protocol, `NoLongerEvil-SelfHosted` for the bits the doc doesn't cover (weather, upload, pro_info), and `emulated_hue` in HA core for the AppRunner/TCPSite pattern.

## 13. Order of work

**Free tests first**

1. Install `tronikos/nest_legacy` via HACS and see if the thermostat appears. Ten minutes.
2. Block the thermostat's internet at the router. Stops the log uploads today.
3. Port-scan it (`00-preflight.sh`). Nobody has published a scan of a stock gen 2.

**Get in**

4. Write down every setting. Photograph every screen.
5. Charge it for an hour.
6. Stand up his server (add-on or Docker). Confirm it's up *before* you flash.
7. Flash. Expect to retry.
8. Point it at his server. Confirm entities appear. **This is now your known-good baseline.**

**Own it**

9. Capture the traffic between the thermostat and his server. That's your test fixture.
10. Write your integration against the capture. Point the device at it with one POST to port 8080.
11. Keep his server installed. Switching back is one more POST.

**Then, separately**

12. Wire tap, sensing only, for a week.
13. Compare the wire tap against what the thermostat reports.
14. Only then, if you still want it, add control relays.
